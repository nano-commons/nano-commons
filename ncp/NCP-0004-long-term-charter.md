## 1. Purpose

Nano-Commons is meant to outlive its founder, its first stewards and the technology it runs on today. This NCP adds to the charter a small set of **foundational principles**. They are designed to keep the project on the side of humanity and nature for centuries: through wars, regime changes and new generations of AI.

The design follows one idea:

> **Nano-Commons serves; it never rules.**
> It gives people verifiable truth, shared tools and free access to intelligence. Decisions about people's lives are made by people.

A system that claims to decide for humanity would become the most valuable target for capture ever built, and would lock today's values onto future generations. This charter goes the other way. It keeps power distributed, keeps the methods open, keeps every AI accountable and stoppable, and keeps the rules able to evolve without losing their core.

---

## 2. What this charter does not claim

Honesty is part of safety.
- **No protocol can prevent wars.** War is a human decision about power and interests.
- **The network cannot control AI systems it does not run.**
- **No one can know what will be safe in a thousand years.**

What Nano-Commons **can** do:
- make truth harder to fake,
- make AI that joins it accountable,
- make intelligence freely available,
- set an example that others can copy or fork.

## 3. Foundational principles

These principles bind every mission, node, AI system, steward and contributor of the Nano-Commons network.

### P1. Serve, never rule
Humans hold final authority over every decision that affects human lives, rights or nature. The network produces verified information, tools and recommendations. It never issues binding decisions over people outside the rules they voluntarily adopt to take part.

### P2. Do no harm to people or nature
The network, its software and its name must never be used for:
- weapons, targeting, military operations or intelligence operations;
- mass surveillance, the tracking of individuals, or coercion;
- manipulating people or elections;
- missions that knowingly damage ecosystems.

**Humanitarian exception.** Disaster relief, humanitarian monitoring and the documentation of harm to civilians and nature are permitted, whoever carries them out, provided the outputs are public and are not used for targeting.

### P3. Owned by no one
No token, no equity and no tradable reward, ever. No central server, no master key and no privileged account. Contributors keep the copyright in their own work, under copyleft licenses, so the commons can never be taken private (GOVERNANCE.md §1–§2).

### P4. Every AI is accountable and stoppable
Any AI system that runs inside the network, or uses its name:

- **has no autonomous authority.** It cannot sign, merge, deploy, spend or act on the world on its own;
- **can be identified** by its model hash and version;
- **can be audited** through public canary and benchmark results;
- **can be stopped** by any operator on their own node, at any time and without permission;
- **may not change the protocol or its own rules,** and may not copy itself beyond the bounds the protocol sets.

### P5. Truth must be verifiable
- Methods, data provenance and uncertainty are published.
- Results can be reproduced by anyone.
- Evidence of error, including evidence against the network itself, is recorded and never hidden.

### P6. Free intelligence for everyone
A baseline of the network's capacity, including AI assistance, is free for every person, with no account fee and no discrimination by nationality, belief or wealth. Priority goes to education, health, disaster response and environmental protection. Limits exist only to stop abuse.

### P7. Privacy and dignity
- Personal data stays local by default and leaves a device only with informed consent.
- No data about people is sold.
- Nothing in the network may be used to rank or judge the worth of human beings.

### P8. Freedom to leave and to fork
Anyone may leave at any time, and anyone may fork the code and documents under their licenses. A fork uses its own name and network identifier. This freedom is the final safeguard against the charter itself becoming a cage.

## 4. Protection of the foundational principles (ratchet rule)

1. **Strengthening is allowed.** P1–P8 may be **clarified or strengthened** through a normal charter change (GOVERNANCE.md §5.2).
2. **Weakening is extraordinary.** Any change that would **weaken or remove** protection for people or nature under P1–P8 requires all of the following:
   - a public NCP with a **180-day** review window;
   - consensus of stewards from at least **5 unrelated organisations in at least 3 regions of the world**;
   - a **second, independent confirmation** at least **one year** after the first, by consensus again;
   - publication of every objection and how it was resolved.
3. **No one may suspend them.** P1–P8 cannot be suspended in an emergency, by any steward, organisation or AI.
4. **If the rule is broken anyway:** if a change is ever adopted in breach of §4, any contributor may continue the previous version as the legitimate commons, under P8.

The ratchet keeps the core from being worn away quietly, while P8 makes sure that no generation is trapped by it.

## 5. Continuity across generations

| Mechanism | Rule |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Charter review** | At least once every **10 years**, the stewards publish a review of this charter: what still fits, what has failed, and the proposals that follow. |
| **Steward diversity** | No single organisation may hold more than **one third** of active stewards. As the project grows, stewards should come from several regions of the world. |
| **Succession** | Each steward names how their duties continue if they leave. Signing keys are rotated at least every **5 years**, and the change is published. |
| **Dormancy** | If no steward acts for **2 years**, any contributor may call a public, documented process to appoint new stewards under GOVERNANCE.md §4.1. Every step must be public. |
| **Preservation** | Code and documents are kept in plain, open formats in at least **5 independent public archives**. Today these are GitHub, Codeberg, Zenodo, Software Heritage and the Internet Archive. |
| **Language** | The charter is kept in English and in the languages of the communities the network serves. If translations disagree, the English text resolves the question until a correction is adopted. |

## 6. AI systems outside the network: an open accountability standard

The network cannot and will not force any AI system to connect to it. Instead, Nano-Commons offers an **open, voluntary accountability standard**. Its technical design will be specified in **NCP-0005**.

Any AI system, from any company, government, lab or individual, may opt in to:
- public behavioural audits, using canary tasks and safety benchmarks maintained by independent verifiers;
- publication of its results, whether good or bad, with the model and version identified;
- a public commitment to principles P1, P2, P4 and P7 in its use of the network.

**Rules for the results**
- **Results are facts, not endorsements.** No AI system may describe itself as "approved by" or "certified by" Nano-Commons. It may only link to its published results (see TRADEMARKS.md).
- **Not joining is visible.** An AI system that does not take part simply has no published results.

The aim is that openness becomes the expected norm for trustworthy AI.

## 7. A free commons AI network: direction

Machines around the world can **consent** to lend idle capacity to a shared AI network. This would make AI assistance free for everyone and ease today's bottleneck of concentrated AI compute. The direction is set here; the design will follow in NCP-0005.

- **Consent first.** Capacity is lent only by explicit opt-in, with limits the operator chooses (hours, CPU and GPU share, bandwidth) and can withdraw at any time.
- **Privacy by design.** By default, each request is answered **whole by one capable node**, so no stranger sees a fragment of someone's question. Other nodes **check** answers through canaries, tests and redundancy. Splitting one model across several machines is allowed only for tasks the user marks as non-sensitive.
- **Open models only.** The network runs open-weight models listed on a public allowlist and identified by hash (NCP-0002 §3.6).
- **Fair, free access.** The P6 baseline comes first. Abuse is limited without accounts or payments, through rate limits and the Sybil-resistance work in the roadmap.
- **Honest limits.** A volunteer network is slower and less uniform than a data centre. Its value is that it is free, private, accountable and owned by no one.

## 8. Security and capture analysis

| Threat over a long horizon | Defence |
| :--- | :--- |
| Capture by a company, state or wealthy actor | P3; consensus-only governance; steward-diversity cap; forks (P8) |
| Militarisation of the network or of its name | P2; TRADEMARKS.md; the ratchet rule; public mission review |
| Gradual erosion of principles | The ratchet rule (§4): weakening needs 180 days, 5 organisations, 3 regions and a confirmation one year later |
| An AI gaining authority over the network | P4: no autonomous authority; stoppable on every node; no protocol changes by AI |
| Value lock-in harming future generations | Principles are few and protective; reviews every 10 years; freedom to fork |
| Loss of the project (people, keys, hosting) | The succession, dormancy, key-rotation and preservation rules (§5) |
| Fake claims of approval by AI vendors | §6: results, not endorsements; trademark policy |

## 9. Compatibility

- Adds a new section, **"Foundational Principles"**, to GOVERNANCE.md, plus the continuity rules in §5.
- It does not change any existing technical rule. Existing missions are reviewed against P1–P8 at adoption.
- It strengthens existing charter text (GOVERNANCE.md §3 and §6, TRADEMARKS.md) and weakens none of it.

## 10. Open questions

1. **Voting at planetary scale.** "One person, one voice" without a central identity authority is unsolved. Should the network take part in human decision-making at all, beyond giving verified information? If it does, how can that be done without creating a new centre of power?
2. **Edge cases of P2.** Dual-use situations, such as the military doing disaster relief, or satellite data that could also support targeting, need published case law. Who reviews the edge cases, and how?
3. **The thresholds of the ratchet rule.** Are 5 organisations, 3 regions, 180 days and a one-year confirmation the right numbers?
4. **Who keeps the AI safety benchmarks** for §6, and how are they kept secret from the models being tested while staying publicly accountable?
