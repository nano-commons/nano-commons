Nano-Commons

An open network that can tell when its own majority is lying.

Nano-Commons lets many strangers produce data and knowledge that can be trusted. Results are signed, cross-checked across regions, and audited against questions whose answers are already known. No one owns it, and no one controls it.

  • No token, ever
  • No central server
  • No master key
  • Serve, never rule

Early stage. Version 0.2 is a reference implementation that runs as a testbed on one machine. It is not yet a live network.

The problem

Intelligence is becoming infrastructure, but the infrastructure is owned by a few and verified by almost no one.

Concentrated power

A few organisations control the compute, the models and the rules for who gets access.

Trust by assertion

Users are asked to believe answers they cannot check, from systems they cannot inspect.

Majorities can lie

Open networks usually treat agreement as truth. A coordinated group with enough weight can make a falsehood "verified".

How it works

Small agents do bounded work. The network checks the work, and then checks itself.

  1. Signed tasksA mission owner signs each task with an Ed25519 key, binding the exact input data and the workers allowed to judge it.
  2. A strict admission gateEvery node runs 15 checks before doing any work: tampering, replay, stale history and exhausted budgets are all refused.
  3. Weighted, capped consensusSeveral independent nodes vote, weighted by earned reputation. No region can hold more than half of the weight, so none can decide alone.
  4. Canary auditsSome tasks are tests with known answers that look exactly like real work. Nodes are scored against the truth, not against the majority.
  5. Correctable memoryEvery accepted result is linked into a hash chain that records who produced it. When a coalition is caught, its results are flagged and can be rolled back.

Principles

The long-term charter, proposed as draft NCP-0004, sets one rule above all others: Nano-Commons serves; it never rules.

PrincipleWhat it means
P1 Serve, never ruleHumans keep final authority over decisions about human lives, rights and nature.
P2 Do no harmNo weapons, targeting, military or intelligence operations, mass surveillance or election manipulation. Humanitarian work is allowed if its outputs are public.
P3 Owned by no oneNo token, no equity, no central server. Contributors keep their copyright under copyleft licences.
P4 Accountable AIEvery AI in the network has no authority of its own, can be identified and audited, and can be stopped by any operator.
P5 Verifiable truthMethods and evidence are public, including evidence against the network itself.
P6 Free intelligenceA baseline of capacity, including AI assistance, is free for everyone.
P7 Privacy and dignityPersonal data stays local by default. No data about people is sold.
P8 Freedom to forkAnyone may leave or fork at any time. That freedom is the final safeguard.

What it cannot do

Honesty is part of safety.

  • It cannot prevent wars. It can make the truth harder to fake.
  • It cannot control AI systems it does not run. It can offer them an open, voluntary audit.
  • Tiny agents are not as capable as large models. Their strength is that they can be verified.
  • Peer-to-peer networking and sandboxing are not built yet.
  • Sybil resistance and independent verifiers are open problems.
  • Changes to the protocol wait until the project has at least three stewards from at least two organisations.

Get involved

Everyone who contributes becomes one of its owners, under the same rules as everyone else.

Run your own node console

git clone https://github.com/nano-commons/nano-commons
cd nano-commons
pip install .
nano-commons serve

The console controls only your machine. There is no central Nano-Commons server. Usage guide

Other ways to help

  • Become a steward: review changes and record consensus.
  • Review a proposal: every change is decided in the open.
  • Contribute code or documents, signed off under the DCO.
  • Report a vulnerability privately.
